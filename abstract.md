## Abstract

Immunotherapy is a fundamental strategy in cancer treatment that harnesses the immune system to identify and suppress tumour growth. Based on the uncontrollable nature of tumour cells in the absence of an immune response and treatment, their growth is assumed to be exponential.
Effector T-cells primarily recognise and eliminate such tumour cells. Immune-checkpoint inhibitors belong to a class of immunotherapy drugs that enable passive or exhausted T-cells to inhibit tumour growth further. However, Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (ICIs), such as Anti-PD-1, are notoriously unpredictable, with low long-term response rates among patients.
Seemingly stable patients can experience aggressive tumour relapse without warning. Even slight biological variations can weaken immune response over time. Although the outcome is often assumed to vary linearly with dosage, in non-linear biological systems, the response is not proportional. 

We develop a non-linear system of differential equations that models the dynamic interactions between tumour cells and effector T-cells. We further implement Bifurcation analysis to predict the system's behaviour.
We also attempt to study variation in the response of the inhibitor drugs as parameters such as killing efficiency, tumour growth rate, and exhaustion rate vary, as they do in different persons. 
This helps us predict the person-specific threshold dosages.

**Keywords:** tumour growth, non-linear dynamics, Immune-checkpoint analysis, Bifurcation analysis
